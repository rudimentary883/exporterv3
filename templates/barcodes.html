1. Names and six-digit identifiers

One person per line: the name and the six-digit identifier printed under the barcode on the tab site's check-in page (Edit Database → Check-Ins has them as a table you can copy). Paste straight from Google Sheets, or upload a .csv. The first line may be a header such as name, identifier.

2. Barcode picture

SVG applies to the ZIP. Spreadsheets always hold PNG pictures: Excel, Google Sheets and Canva cannot read SVG inside a cell.

Same barcode, more pixels (PNG). The shape never changes.

Both put the barcode in the barcode column of its row. If Canva does not see the pictures in one style, try the other.

Nothing is stored: the file is made on the spot and sent to you.

Using the XLSX in Canva

  1. Open your name-tag design and add an image frame where the barcode goes (340 × 100 px, ratio 3.4 : 1).
  2. Apps → Bulk create → upload this XLSX.
  3. Right-click the frame → Connect data → choose the barcode column. Connect the name to your name text box as usual.

If the barcode column is not offered as images: download the XLSX in the other style, or build the column in Google Sheets with the fillBarcodeColumn() function of the Apps Script (File → Download → .xlsx), which is the route Canva's help describes for Google Sheets. The ZIP holds one <identifier>.png (or .svg) per person plus names.csv.
